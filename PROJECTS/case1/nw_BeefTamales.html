Beef Tamales

by The Albas and Nieves Christmas Experiments

A "trial by fire" recipe built from 2 families experimenting. You can make hundreds, or two, depending on how you feel (we measure by the heart).

Ingredients

  • Corn Husks

  • The Masa

  • Lard
  • Masa
  • Chicken Bouillion
  • Baking Powder
  • Warm Water

  • The Filling

  • Chuck Roast
  • Chiles: Guajillo, California and Ancho
  • Onions
  • Garlic
  • Oregano
  • Bay Leaf
  • Salt and Pepper
  • Optional: 1 can of Ancho Chile in Adobo Sauce (spicy)

Directions

    The Corn Husks

  1. Soak the Corn husks in water overnight, or at minimum in warm water for 1 hour.

    2. Masa

  2. Mix the masa with Chicken Boullion to taste (should be slightly salty), and lard to make a crumble mixture. Add approx. 1 tbsp. of baking powder for large batches, use good judgement if you are making smaller batches
  3. Begin adding warm water and mixing until the consistency of a runny spackling paste (you should be able to "paint" it onto your palm)
  4. Let it rest, while you make the meat portion. You can also make a sweet filling, but for this recipe we are using beef.

    5. The Filling

  5. Boil water. While this starts, deseed the chiles by ripping off the stems and removing seeds. Cut Onion in half and remove the outer skins.
  6. Boil Chiles, onions, garlic, 2 bay leaves, and oregano (measure by the heart. If it smells good, it will taste good) for approx. 15 minutes or until the chiles are soft.
  7. Remove the boiled spices from the water and put in a blender to blend. Slowly add water until a thick sauce is made.
  8. Strain the liquid, and use this to pressure cook the chuck roast until it falls apart.
  9. *optional* you can use Ancho Chile in adobo sauce, but use sparingly as that spice lingers.
  10. shred the meat, and let it cool in the sauce.

    Assembly

  11. Take a hydrated corn husk and spread the masa on the husk. If it is crumbly, re-mix the masa with some more water.
  12. fill a small amound of beef in the center of the husk, along the length of it.
  13. fold the husk in thirds, overlapping, and then fold the bottom tip upwards. You can also use strips of corn husk to tie around to keep it in place
  14. In a large pot with a steamer base, you will boil water.
  15. Place the tamales with the folded tip down (open face up) and steam them for 20-40 minutes until it separates from the husk
  16. Let them cool and top them with favorite toppings, such as salsa, cilantro, cheese, etc.
  17. Freeze them in freezer bags to enjoy for the year or share with friends.

It might have taken us 5 years to figure it out, but they are GOOD.
